Ancient Greek Philosophy

Socrates (470-399 BCE)

Socrates is considered one of the founders of Western philosophical thought. His method of questioning to arrive at truth, known as the Socratic method, remains influential today. His famous quote "I know that I know nothing" exemplifies his approach to wisdom and understanding.

Plato (428-348 BCE)

Student of Socrates and teacher of Aristotle, Plato founded the Academy in Athens. His works, written in dialogue form, explore justice, beauty, and equality. His "Theory of Forms" suggests that the physical world is not the 'real' world; instead, ultimate reality exists beyond our physical world.

Aristotle (384-322 BCE)

Aristotle was a student of Plato who became one of the most influential philosophers. He wrote about logic, metaphysics, mathematics, physics, biology, botany, ethics, politics, agriculture, medicine, dance, and theatre. His concept of logic dominated this field for 2000 years.
